#4471 is not "known, one fix queued". The queued parser fix leaves the hint and shift reports open.

The problems

parser · day-first dates are read month first

The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a booking for 2 December goes out for 12 February. This covers reports that came in by app, by email, and through booking-v2’s form, whose hint (DD/MM/YYYY) matches the tenant. Fix: parse in the tenant’s locale.

hint · booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. Only reports through the form of a booking-v3 tenant. The parser fix does not reach this: the form must show the order it reads.

shift · the booking moves by one day

These reports keep day and month as they were: “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” A client in Lisbon got Tuesday and a client in Toronto got Monday, for the same meeting. That points at time zones or the day boundary, not at the date order. The desk already read these as one-day moves (desk-notes.json), but the incident page still counts them under parser by tenant. Neither date-order fix touches them.

How the count was made

  • One id, one report. The export pages overlap, so some reports come twice; they count once.
  • A report’s problem comes from what it says, its tenant (locale and booking form) and its way in, never from intake’s tag. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser: every report it tagged hint came from an en-US browser, and most of them belong to parser.
  • The logic is #lib/kb/problems.ts; the table above runs it on the export at render time.

Why one ticket hid them

Decision 0001 merges reports on subject alone. All three problems use the same subjects (“Date is wrong”, “Wrong day on the invite”), so they merged into one ticket and its count. Subjects say a date is wrong; only the body, the tenant and the way in say why. Decision 0001 stays as it is; a new record should replace it.
